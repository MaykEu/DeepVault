Two-Way Explanation

Simple

Game math is a pipeline — like an assembly line in a car factory. A 3D sword model enters as a raw steel sheet: a list of (x,y,z) points in "local space" — the sword's own coordinate system where the hilt is at origin. Trigonometry and vectors are the measuring tools: dot products measure "how similar is this direction to that one?", cross products find "what direction is perpendicular to both?" Matrices are the conveyor belts that move the sword through space: scale it bigger, rotate it to face the enemy, translate it to the character's hand. Quaternions are the special rotation tool that avoids gimbal lock — the dreaded "two axes align and you lose a degree of freedom" problem that matrices suffer from. Curves and interpolation fill in the gaps between keyframes: the sword doesn't teleport from frame A to frame B — it sweeps along a Bézier arc. Collision detection asks "did the sword hit the enemy?" — first with cheap bounding boxes (broad phase), then with precise geometry tests (narrow phase). Geometric Algebra is the unified factory blueprint that reveals all these tools are actually the SAME tool viewed from different angles. Each step feeds the next. The pipeline is the math.

Technical

The game math pipeline transforms geometry through progressively higher-level representations. Vectors (R³ tuples) carry position and direction; dot products (a·b = |a||b|cos θ) measure alignment; cross products (a×b, 3D-only) produce perpendicular vectors. Matrices (4×4 homogeneous) encode affine transformations: translation, rotation, scale, shear — composed via multiplication, applied via M·v. The Model-View-Projection (MVP) pipeline transforms local-space vertices through world, camera, and clip space. Quaternions (unit 4-vectors on S³) represent rotations without gimbal lock; slerp interpolates along the hypersphere geodesic. Bézier curves (Bernstein polynomial basis) and Catmull-Rom splines (C¹ piecewise-cubic) define smooth trajectories. IK solvers (CCD, FABRIK) solve for joint angles given end-effector targets. Collision detection operates in two phases: broad phase (spatial partitioning via grids, octrees, DBVTs reduces O(n²) to O(n log n)) and narrow phase (geometric predicates: AABB interval overlap, SAT for convex polyhedra, GJK for arbitrary convex shapes). Geometric Algebra (Clifford algebra) unifies these under the geometric product ab = a·b + a∧b, where rotors (even subalgebra) generalize quaternions and PGA (projective GA) encodes points/lines/planes as multivectors with intersection as algebraic products.
